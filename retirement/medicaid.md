# medicaid

Similar to [medicare](medicare.md), medicaid is a health benefit relevant to retirees.  Medicaid is a _joint_ federal-state program, with state-governed eligibility.


## Purpose

According to [medicare's website](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/help/medicaid), medicaid covers health benefits not already covered by medicare; for example, medicaid helps with nursing home and hospice care.  Medicaid is targeted at low-income, disabled, and elderly people.


## Eligibility

Interestingly, it seems like (again, according to [the medicare website](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/help/medicaid)) medicaid eligibility is determined at the state level.

There is currently a scary banner stating that they will start enforcing 80 hours of work per month for some adults to maintain their benefits.  It excludes, however, those who qualify for medicare.

### Missouri

[Missouri's medicaid website](https://mydss.mo.gov/healthcare) (termed MO HealthNet) contains a lot of information.  In particular, there is a wealth of information for services and eligibility on [their application page](https://mydss.mo.gov/healthcare/apply).

It looks like seniors 65+ and older are included, and that there's a supplemental form those already claiming social security will need to fill out.  It looks like it could take up to 45 days for coverage to be determined/granted.

Missouri has a spend-down program, so that you can qualify for medicaid benefits once you've spent enough so that your income is below the eligibility requirements.  They have [an FAQ](https://mydss.mo.gov/mhd/spend-down-faqs).  E.g., if your bills don't put you below the income limits, you won't qualify.  If, however, your bills _do_ put you below the income limits, you'll qualify for MO HealthNet coverage that month.  Now, there is some loose language in the FAQ ("may qualify"), but this is the basic idea they describe.  Curiously, it even goes on to describe using costs from other months to meet the spend down limits for the current month, where it looks like bills can be used for any of the next three months to meet these limits.

According to the [resource limits](https://dssmanuals.mo.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/mhabd-appendix-j.pdf), some of these programs appear to only apply for extreme poverty.  For example, 2 retirees must get less than $1,600 total from social security and have less than $12,100 in total (cash, or liquid) assets.  A single retiree would need less than $1,200 per month on social security benefits and less than $6,100 in liquid assets.  I _believe_ this is where the "spend down" concept comes into play, where [the FAQ](https://mydss.mo.gov/mhd/spend-down-faqs) describes how you can subtract the cost of medical bills from your excess income to qualify for MO HealthNet on a month-to-month basis.

## Benefits

Medicaid appears to help those who cannot even afford the costs associated with medicare.  The [medicare website](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/help/medicaid) lists that full medicaid coverage pays for the medicare premium, deductivles, coinsurance, and copayments.  Full medicaid benefits futher includes [extra help](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/help/drug-costs) with drug costs, and other drug or service costs not covered by medicare.  The initial preamble on their website provides the concrete exmaple of nursing home costs.


## Dual eligibility

According to [the medicare website](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/help/medicaid), those with both medicare and medicaid are "dually eligible," which means that these benefits take turns paying for different costs.  Medicare benefits are used first, and medicaid benefits are used to supplement those costs not covered by medicare.

### Prescription drugs

Medicare is supposed to cover the cost of prescription drugs.  If medicare does not cover your drug needs, medicaid _may_ cover it, and should be considered.
